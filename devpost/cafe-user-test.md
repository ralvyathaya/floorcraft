# Café usability check

Status: **not performed**. No café owner or barista has participated yet. Do not describe this guide or an agent's browser checks as customer validation.

## Purpose

Learn whether a café owner/operator or barista can compare capacity, customer circulation and staff travel, and interpret the model's limits. An architect may additionally comment on conceptual circulation/readability. This is a usability check, not a calibration or building-compliance study.

## Short session

1. Ask what layout/queue problem they have actually encountered. Record their own description and expected consequence; do not supply a desired answer.
2. Let them open FloorCraft, choose a preset, move one object and run the fixed rush hour. Observe whether they understand dragging the floor footprint, edit locks and completion.
3. Try the 12-seat case study: Start example, run A, Apply B and run B. Ask them to explain the trade-off in their own words, using completion, dine-in fulfillment, production stages, barista travel and obstruction. Observe whether Show on tested layout helps locate the recorded obstruction. The attributed public-plan adaptation is a synthetic experiment, not validation of the source café.
4. Let them move a seating group or valid service station, rerun and inspect a Custom variation. Then compare two preset capacities. Ask whether they can separate a capacity change from an arrangement change, and whether lower time with fewer fulfilled dine-in requests meets their goal. Legacy 21/24 takeaway results are not café-model evidence.
5. Ask which part felt confusing, unrealistic or unhelpful, and what they would try next in their own café.

## Evidence to capture

With the participant's permission, record role/context without identifying personal details, task completion/confusion, their own interpretation, one useful decision, and requested corrections. Keep private participant details outside public project documents. Do not invent quotes, participants, success rates or testimonials.

Current evidence remains executable synthetic tests and browser verification. Real-world forecasting would additionally need measured arrivals/service times, observed trajectories and a separate calibration/validation process; that work is outside this PoC.
